## E. Recovery Disposition And Evidence Boundaries

`adopt` means only reintroducing the exact audited blob bytes from a clean,
then-current main branch after explicit approval; it forbids cherry-picking a
whole commit or PR and inherits no historical pass conclusion. There are no
unconditional adopt entries in this audit.

`reimplement` permits reading the audited block to recover intent, risk, and
test boundaries. It requires a new current-contract diff and fresh evidence.
`drop` means no code or contract migration; only the branch, audit, and
failure facts remain reproducible.

P3a-R's technical entrance is limited to current-main verification of version
route, strict-context isolation, canonical/fingerprint, coverage, privacy, and
old-path regression. Synthetic performance/support-limit work remains deferred
and cannot make reconstruction of PR #43 private sessions or snapshots a
P3a-R prerequisite.

A recovery candidate must prove, before any claim of compatibility:

1. Exact canonical bytes and semantic fingerprints do not drift for committed
   current-main routes and goldens that must remain compatible.
2. Current invalid/rejection corpora retain stable error categories, permitted
   text, and error order.
3. A deterministic current-main versus recovery-candidate generated corpus
   has been compared, including canonical, stable-ID, coverage, default-closed,
   semantic, and privacy regressions.

Those checks protect only paths already supported by current main. They do not
prove unavailable old-H formal-scale canonical-byte or fingerprint equivalence;
that equivalence remains unproven. A future task stops when its own required,
available oracle cannot be obtained. That rule does not turn the retired,
unavailable old-H oracle into a recovery precondition.
